# Tenant Improvement Contractor in Portland, Oregon: What to Know Before You Build Out

Leasing a commercial space in Portland is usually the easy part. Turning that empty shell, or someone else's old layout, into a space that actually works for your business is where most of the real decisions get made. That process is called tenant improvement, or TI for short, and it covers everything from knocking out a wall to running new electrical for a restaurant kitchen.

We handle tenant improvement work alongside our residential projects, and the two aren't as different as people assume. A commercial build-out still comes down to good framing, clean mechanical work, and a crew that shows up when they say they will. The scale is bigger and the code requirements shift, but the fundamentals of honest construction don't change just because there's a storefront sign out front instead of a mailbox.

## What Counts as a Tenant Improvement Project

Tenant improvement is a broad category, and that's part of why it confuses people signing their first commercial lease. It can mean a simple cosmetic refresh, new paint, flooring, and lighting, for a retail space that's otherwise ready to go. It can also mean a full gut and rebuild for a restaurant that needs a hood system, grease trap, and an entirely new kitchen layout before the health department will sign off.

Office buildouts tend to land in the middle. You're usually dealing with demising walls, data and electrical runs for workstations, maybe a kitchenette or a couple of private offices, and ADA-compliant restrooms. Medical and dental suites are their own animal entirely, with plumbing and electrical requirements tied to specific equipment. Whatever the use, the work falls under commercial remodeling, which is where [our commercial projects](/project-photoshop) live alongside the rest of our portfolio.

## Why Local Knowledge Matters for TI Work

Portland's permitting process has its own rhythm, and it's not the same as Vancouver, Beaverton, or Gresham. Occupancy classifications, fire separation requirements, and ADA upgrades can all get triggered the moment you change a space's use, and a contractor who hasn't pulled permits with the city recently is going to be guessing at timelines. That guessing turns into delays, and delays turn into rent paid on a space you can't open yet.

A contractor based in the city also tends to have relationships with the same inspectors, the same subcontractors, and the same suppliers project after project. That familiarity doesn't show up on paper, but it shows up in how fast a rough-in inspection gets scheduled or how quickly an electrical sub can get on site when a schedule shifts. We're on SE Hawthorne Blvd, and most of our commercial work stays within Portland and the surrounding area for exactly that reason.

## What a Build-Out Actually Involves

Most TI projects move through a predictable sequence even when the finishes and square footage vary wildly. It starts with a walkthrough of the existing space, usually with the landlord's base building plans in hand if they exist, followed by a scope discussion about what the business actually needs on day one versus what can wait. From there it's design and permitting, then demo, then the rough-in work: framing, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC, all of which has to pass inspection before walls get closed up.

After rough-in comes drywall, flooring, millwork, and fixtures, and then the finish work that makes a space feel finished rather than just built. A fast cosmetic refresh might take a few weeks. A full restaurant build-out with new mechanical systems can run several months once permitting is factored in. Anyone quoting a firm timeline without seeing the space or the permit requirements first is probably rounding down.

We once walked a vacant storefront a short block from our office near Hawthorne Blvd for a client who wanted to open within a tight window tied to their lease terms. The space needed new electrical service and a reworked floor plan, and the biggest holdup wasn't the construction itself, it was waiting on utility coordination that nobody had budgeted time for. It's a good reminder that the construction schedule and the permitting schedule aren't always the same schedule, and a contractor who's done this before will plan for that gap instead of promising around it.

## Budgeting for Tenant Improvements

TI costs vary more than almost any other type of construction project because tenant allowances, lease terms, and existing building conditions all factor in differently from one deal to the next. Some landlords offer a TI allowance that covers part or all of the build-out, which changes what the tenant needs to finance directly. Others offer bare shell space with no allowance at all, which means every wall, outlet, and light fixture comes out of the business owner's pocket.

Because of that variation, we'd rather walk a space and talk through actual scope than throw out a number that doesn't mean anything yet. Ask about your lease's TI allowance language before you sign, and bring that document to your first meeting with a contractor. It changes the conversation about what's realistic for your opening timeline and your budget.

## Choosing a TI Contractor

The questions worth asking a tenant improvement contractor aren't that different from the ones you'd ask for any remodel, just scaled up. Ask how many commercial permits they've pulled in Portland recently, ask who handles the electrical and plumbing subcontracting, and ask what happens if an inspection fails and the schedule slips. A contractor who answers those plainly, without dodging into vague reassurances, is usually the one who's done this enough times to know where the actual risks are.

It also helps to ask about communication during the build. Commercial projects tend to have more moving parts than a home remodel, more subs on site at once, more coordination with the landlord, and more pressure tied to an opening date. You want a point of contact who answers the phone and tells you the truth about delays before they become a surprise.

## FAQ

### What is a tenant improvement contractor?
A tenant improvement contractor builds out leased commercial space to fit a specific business's needs, handling everything from permits to finish work. This differs from residential remodeling mainly in the code requirements and the coordination involved with landlords and lease terms.

### How long does a tenant improvement project take in Portland?
Timelines depend heavily on scope and permitting, ranging from a few weeks for a cosmetic refresh to several months for a full restaurant or medical build-out. Utility coordination and inspection scheduling often affect the timeline as much as the actual construction work.

### Who pays for tenant improvements, the landlord or the tenant?
It depends on the lease. Some landlords offer a TI allowance that covers part or all of the build-out costs, while other leases put the full cost on the tenant, so it's worth reviewing your lease language closely before budgeting.
